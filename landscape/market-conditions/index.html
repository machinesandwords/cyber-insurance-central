Current Market Conditions: Soft Pricing, Hard Underwriting

As of Q1 2026. Pricing and underwriting conditions shift on their own cycle, separate from the structural facts covered on the landscape overview. This page is reviewed and updated quarterly.

The first quarter of 2026 marked the eighth consecutive quarter of pricing declines in the US cyber insurance market. On its own, that number suggests buyers have the upper hand.

The underwriting side tells a different story. Buyers now face a wider range of exclusions and higher retentions, which are the amounts a policyholder pays out of pocket before coverage kicks in, than they did even two years ago. Multi-factor authentication, tested offline backups, and endpoint detection have moved from recommended practice to underwriting prerequisites, and carriers increasingly want evidence those controls are in place, not just an attestation that they are.

Organizations that meet that bar are benefiting from the price declines. Organizations that don't are seeing the opposite: premium increases well above market average, non-renewals, and tighter sublimits, which are how much a policy will pay for a specific type of loss within the overall coverage amount.

Forecasters disagree on what comes next. Some expect the two-year softening trend to continue. Others expect a reversal, pointing to rising claim severity and the cost of AI-driven attacks, with premium increases in the range of 15 to 20 percent forecast for 2026. Neither outcome changes the underlying shift away from the pricing cycle and toward demonstrable controls: underwriting has gotten more selective regardless of which way pricing moves, and a buyer renewing in a soft quarter with weak controls will still fare worse than one renewing in a hard quarter with strong controls.
